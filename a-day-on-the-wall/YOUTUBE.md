## Description

It's AD 130. You're Gaius, 26, a legionary of the Sixth Legion, posted to Hadrian's Wall: 73 miles of stone across the north of Britain, the edge of the Roman world.

Live one whole day on the Wall, from the trumpet before dawn to the third watch of the night. Porridge for breakfast (again). A centurion with a vine stick. Frost-cracked stone to rebuild with your own hands. A letter home asking for socks and underpants. A bath, a toll gate, and the locals Rome calls "little Brits".

But four scouts went north two days ago, and they haven't come back.

The history is real:
- Hadrian's Wall was begun in AD 122, ran 80 Roman miles (73 miles) coast to coast, with a small fort (milecastle) every Roman mile and two turrets between.
- It was built by legionaries of the II, VI and XX legions. "Centurial stones" carved with a century's name mark the stretches they built.
- Wooden letters found at Vindolanda, near the Wall, include a soldier receiving socks, two pairs of sandals and two pairs of underpants, an officer writing that his men have no beer, and a report calling the locals "Brittunculi".
- Eight men shared one barrack room; soldiers paid for their own food and kit out of their wages.
- The scouts, the trader and the lights in the fog are fiction.
