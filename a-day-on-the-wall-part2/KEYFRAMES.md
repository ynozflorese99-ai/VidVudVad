# A Day on the Wall, Part 2: "The Night the Lights Came" (key frames for review)

**Style:** the same as Part 1: thick oil paint laid with a palette knife, the same narrator, and the same Gaius.
**Length:** about 10–11 minutes, in chapters, with a painted title card for each hour of the night.
**New: burned-in subtitles.** Each narration line appears on screen in white text on a soft dark box at the bottom of the frame (see `keyframes/subtitle_sample.jpg`). You'll still get the separate `.srt` file for YouTube as well.

## The story
Part 2 starts the moment you blow the horn. The signal fires race along the Wall, and the century turns out in the dark. Then the lights on the moor go out. You wait in the rain until dawn. When the cavalry ride out, they find where the lights came from: a trampled ring where fifty men stood waiting, and three torches stuck in the ground. Two miles further on, in a shepherd's hut, they find one of the four missing scouts, alive. What he tells them explains everything: the tribes were testing how fast Rome would answer. The horn answered them. The film ends at sunrise, with a true history hook: fifty years later, the tribes did cross the Wall.

The history in it is real:
- the Wall's signal system
- auxiliary cavalry from the forts
- scouts (*exploratores*) working beyond the Wall
- the tribes north of the Wall
- Cassius Dio's account of the tribes crossing the Wall around AD 180
