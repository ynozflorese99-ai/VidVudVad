## The history in this episode
- Hadrian's Wall had a turret every third of a Roman mile and a milecastle every mile, built so that each could see the next. Exactly how the signalling worked is still debated.
- Most of the men garrisoning the Wall were **auxiliaries**: non-citizens recruited across the Empire, from Gaul, Germany and Spain, and even Hamian archers from Syria at Carvoran. After 25 years' service they received Roman citizenship for themselves and their children.
- The **Vindolanda tablets** are thin wooden writing tablets found at the fort of Vindolanda: strength reports, lists of the sick, requests for supplies (beer included). One is a note on how the Britons fight: no armour, many cavalry, calling them *Brittunculi*, "wretched little Britons".
- **Cassius Dio** records that early in Commodus' reign (around AD 180) "the tribes in the island crossed the wall that separated them from the Roman forts" and cut down a general together with his troops.
- Gaius, Felix, Rufus and the night of the lights are fiction, set in the real world of the Wall.
