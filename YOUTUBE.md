## Description

It's 11:40 p.m. on a Tuesday. You're the night operator at the Harlow Creek water treatment plant, and 40,000 people drink what you send them.

At 11:46, the radio says the word "outbreak" three times. Then it plays music. Your relief never shows up.

For 30 days, you keep the water running alone. You learn how far a tower of water can carry a town, how many days of diesel you have left, and what the smart meter at your sister's house says every morning at 7 a.m.

But every night at 2:14 a.m., the door at the river intake opens. And it closes again 30 seconds later.

The water facts are real:
- Gravity, not pumps, pushes water into most homes. Every 2.31 feet of water in a tower adds about 1 psi of pressure.
- Utilities treat pressure below 20 psi as a contamination risk, because groundwater can be drawn into the pipes. That's why it can trigger a boil-water notice.
- Chlorine has to last all the way to the farthest tap. Leaves, mud and algae "use it up"; operators call this chlorine demand.
- Liquid chlorine (sodium hypochlorite) loses strength over time, so plants get regular deliveries.
