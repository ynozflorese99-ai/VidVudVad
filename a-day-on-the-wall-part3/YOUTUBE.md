## The history in this episode
- **Marching camps:** Roman armies dug a defended camp every night on campaign, with a ditch, a bank and a stake palisade, and tents in regular rows. Hundreds of their outlines are known in Scotland, many found from the air.
- **Burnswark Hill** (Dumfries and Galloway): an Iron Age hillfort with two Roman camps at its foot. Excavations found hundreds of Roman lead sling bullets. Whether it was a real siege or a training ground is still debated.
- **Lucco, in charge of pigs:** a real Vindolanda tablet mentions *Lucco ad porcos*. Our Lucco is fiction; his name is borrowed from that tablet.
- **Slings** were a deadly weapon in both Roman and British hands.
- **Retirement:** after 25 years, Roman soldiers received an honourable discharge and a payment. Many settled near their old posts, married locally and raised families.
- **Cassius Dio** records the tribes crossing the Wall around AD 180. Rome's administration of Britain ended around AD 410.
- Gaius, Felix, Rufus, Titus, Verus, Lucco and the rescue are fiction, set in the real world of the Wall.
